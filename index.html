The Robot

One Robot, Every Role
a Museum Needs

India holds thousands of years of history — and far too few hands to guide, map and preserve it all. Yantrax is our answer.

Inside a museum, Yantrax drives itself between exhibits, greets visitors it detects along the way, and explains each artifact through speech and an interactive touchscreen — in the visitor’s own Indian language. At archaeological sites, the same robot switches roles: it maps the terrain, records what it sees, and turns fragile handwritten manuscripts into searchable digital text with its onboard OCR.

No internet. No operator. One intelligent platform, preserving culture while it guides the people who came to experience it.

Every year, manuscripts fade, inscriptions weather away, and stories go untold because there aren’t enough guides, archivists and surveyors to go around. Our mission is to put intelligent robotics to work where human hands are scarce — guiding visitors, mapping sites and digitizing documents before time takes them.

We imagine museums where every visitor is welcomed in their own language, and archaeological sites that are mapped and archived as carefully as they were built. Yantrax is our first step toward that vision: technology that doesn’t replace the human connection to heritage, but multiplies it.

Offline Intelligence

Every Ability, Onboard.
No Internet Required.

Everything Yantrax knows runs on a single NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano. Autonomous navigation steers it through crowded halls; computer vision spots visitors and artifacts; speech recognition understands questions in multiple Indian languages and answers in kind. Because none of it needs the cloud, Yantrax works just as well in a basement archive as in a modern gallery.

The same platform carries an OCR pipeline that photographs historical manuscripts and converts them into digital, searchable text — and a mapping stack that surveys archaeological sites as it moves. Touch, voice, vision and motion, fused into one machine that any museum can simply switch on.
